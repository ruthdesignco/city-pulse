## Focus question answers

**1. Licensing and attribution.** Ordered by severity:
1. The 511 credit wording and link (§5.b). This is a breach today.
2. The §2.g notice to MTC, due by about 2026-11-18: not yet delivered; resend pending, due 2026-11-18 (correction 2026-10-22: the first send bounced).
3. An ODbL statement for `city.bin` in the repo. The on-site OSM credit itself is correct.
4. A 511 notice on `routes.json`. Redistribution is allowed (§1), but subject to the agreement (§2.c).
5. A code LICENSE and the three.js MIT notices.

Already fine:
- DataSF is PDDL, so the credit is a courtesy.
- Landmark materials cite Wikipedia for facts, with no text copied.
- "Muni · live" is descriptive and uses no logos.
- Nothing is branded "511-something" (§5.c).

**2. Mid-range laptops.**
- **Load:** about 1.4 MB gzip in total. `city.bin` (831 KB gzip) only starts downloading after the 375 KB JS chunk has parsed and React has mounted. Building the city takes about 226 ms on the M4 Pro, likely 0.6–1 s on a mid-range CPU (estimate). Roughly 2–3 s to buildings on Wi-Fi (estimate).
- **Memory:** about 300–450 MB of GPU memory (estimate), which is fine on every target.
- **What breaks first:** fill rate, from full-resolution SSAO, 4x MSAA in half-float and the normal-pass re-render. Then vertex cost: the 566k-triangle city is drawn about four times a frame (shadow, main, normal pass, water reflection). Then, on integrated GPUs under memory pressure, a lost context.
- **Graceful fallback:** none below "no WebGL". Fix 5 adds one.

**3. Accuracy and honesty.**
- **Real data:** footprints, tagged heights, trees and Muni positions. Interpolation is fine because freshness is shown.
- **Implies more than the data supports:**
  - Fixed golden-hour light under a "live" label.
  - Procedural storefronts, rooftop equipment and stone-vs-glass mixed in with real buildings. Viewers will read a storefront as a real shop.
  - Default 8 m heights on 19% of buildings, which read as real low-rises.
  - Vehicles scaled up to 3x, and cable cars not in their real colors. Both deliberate and recorded in SPEC, but not disclosed on screen.
- **Fine:** trees nudged up to 2.6 m to the curb, within the inventory's own location error. Skipped median trees are recorded in SPEC.
- **Fix 7 covers it**, along with time of day as the first Later item.

**4. Demo readiness.** Not ready to record yet:
- Shot 5 has no recording.
- The facts on the shot list are stale.
- The 511 credit, which is in every frame, breaches the agreement.
- 60 fps while recording hasn't been checked.

The smallest set before recording is fixes 1, 6, 4 and 8. Then record from the live URL with `?demo` at golden hour, as the shot list says. Quinn: lead any post with the video, with the live link second. The video absorbs the curious, and Hobby bandwidth goes to those who stay.

**Later list order.** The board agrees on the ends and disagrees about the middle:
1. **Real time of day.** Unanimous. No new live dependency, it fixes the clearest honesty gap, and `?time=` makes shots repeatable. Ship the real sun angle first; night with lit windows can follow.
2. **NWS fog and clouds** (Osmani, Hightower). Fetch it server-side on pg_cron into the view, never per tab (Quinn). It needs its own freshness label, plus the fix-5 quality floor, since height fog costs per pixel.
3. **Ferries and wind.** Ferries share the 511 key's 60-per-hour budget with Muni, which already uses about 45, so they need the higher limit first (Quinn, Majors). Wind reuses the NWS source.
4. **Pedestrians.** Last: the most new modelling for the least truth, and the heaviest on performance and privacy. Majors would move them to second, but only if the ridership aggregate is baked and static, since that adds no live dependency.

## Where the board agrees
- The 511 wording, the scorecard scrub and proving the rotation come first. They're cheap, and two of them are publicly visible.
- A public repo needs a code license and a data-licenses section. `city.bin` must be stated as ODbL.
- The site needs a floor for weak GPUs. That means a capped set of automatic step-downs with no settings UI, not a quality-tier system. Osmani narrowed his ask to four steps, and Hightower's objection was to a "system", not to the steps.
- A lost context must not leave the pill saying "live".
- Real time of day is the next feature.
- Not now: a staging project, baking in CI, splitting the bundle, a CDN strategy, or load testing on Hobby.

## Disagreements
1. **Client Sentry now.** Majors vs Hightower, Quinn and Hunt.
   - Majors: without it, the most likely failure on a public site, a weak GPU or a lost context, is invisible, and the weak-GPU fallback is a fallback, not a signal. She'd cap it at one event per session and three event types (boundary error, context lost, fell to the lowest step), tagged with GPU and fps.
   - Hightower: a third vendor for a demo maybe forty people open. Put the failure on screen instead (dim the pill, "graphics reset"), plus the uptime checks.
   - Quinn: a DSN in a public bundle is a public write endpoint, so client-side sampling doesn't protect the quota. Any cap has to live in Sentry's dashboard, with spike protection and a hard monthly quota.
   - Hunt: today the site collects nothing about visitors, so nothing about them can leak. Adding a third-party processor adds browser fingerprints and possibly URLs. If it ships: `sendDefaultPii` off and query strings stripped.
2. **Branch protection on `main`.** Willison vs Hightower.
   - Willison: this repo has two committers, Tyler and an auto-mode agent with push rights that read untrusted content this session. Blocking force-pushes and requiring CI is a deterministic rule the agent can't talk its way past.
   - Hightower: ceremony for a solo developer.
3. **Client poll interval.** Quinn vs Kleppmann.
   - Quinn: 30 s to 60 s halves egress, and buses are interpolated anyway.
   - Kleppmann: a 60 s poll on top of the 20 s server tick puts a healthy bus's on-screen age near 155 s, too close to the 3-minute stale threshold. Confirm the caps first, and change the interval only if measured egress requires it, with a test that "slow" still reads differently from "stale".
4. **Where pedestrians fall in the Later list.** Majors would put them second, if they're static; everyone else puts them last.

## Strongest dissent
Majors: the board is agreeing to a fallback that nobody will ever see work.
- If the site quietly drops to the lowest step on a visitor's laptop, or goes black after a lost context, Tyler learns nothing: not which GPUs struggle, not how often, not whether the fix helped.
- "Show it on screen" only reaches the visitor, who will close the tab.
- For a public portfolio piece whose most likely failure is on the viewer's machine, one capped event stream is the minimum needed to know it works.

What would change her mind:
- A one-time measurement on real mid-range hardware showing the lowest step holds 30 fps or better.
- An uptime check that loads the page in a real browser and verifies the canvas renders, so a deploy that breaks rendering alerts without client telemetry.
